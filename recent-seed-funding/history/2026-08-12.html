What they do
  • Sell to utilities, energy developers, and grid planners.
  • AI simulation of electrical grids for capacity planning and interconnection.
  • Helps model what happens when new loads (data centers, EVs) hit the grid.
The problem they solve
  • Grid interconnection studies take months. New AI data centers need answers fast.
  • Manual power-flow modeling can't keep up with demand from AI buildout.
  • Example: a data center developer models grid impact before submitting to the utility.
Money
  • B2B SaaS for energy.
  • Led by Powerhouse Ventures and Pale Blue Dot, with Stepchange.
Moat
For:
  • Grid modeling requires physics + ML. Not easy to wrapper-ify.
  • AI data center boom is driving urgent demand for this exact tool.
Against:
  • PSS/E (Siemens), PowerWorld, GE are legacy grid simulation incumbents.
  • $2.5M is tiny for a compute-heavy simulation product.
Verdict: 🟡 Good timing (AI energy demand). Hard technical problem.
Crowding
  • PSS/E (Siemens), PowerWorld, GE, Gridcog ($10M Series A).
  • Low in AI-native grid simulation. High in legacy grid tools.
7. Adaptive Insurance — AI-driven specialty climate insurance for property
$5M seed (Jul 2026)
What they do
  • An MGA and insurtech providing AI-driven specialty climate insurance for climate resilience.
  • Sell to property owners, developers, and commercial real estate operators.
  • Uses AI to underwrite climate-related risk (flood, wildfire, wind).
The problem they solve
  • Traditional insurers are pulling out of climate-vulnerable markets.
  • Property owners can't get coverage or face 200%+ premium increases.
  • Example: a CRE owner in a flood zone gets AI-priced coverage when incumbents won't quote.
Money
  • MGA model — earns underwriting commission + premium share.
  • Backed by Connecticut Innovations, Seraphim, Congruent Ventures.
Moat
For:
  • MGA license + reinsurance relationships = regulatory moat.
  • Proprietary climate risk models trained on property-level data.
  • Incumbents retreating from the market = supply vacuum.
Against:
  • Kettle, Descartes, Arbol are funded climate-risk insurtechs.
  • Underwriting losses from extreme weather can sink a small MGA.
Verdict: 🟡 Timing is right (insurer retreat). Tail risk is real.
Crowding
  • Kettle ($50M+ raised), Descartes Underwriting, Arbol, Neptune Flood.
  • Moderate in AI climate insurance. Low in pure-play property climate MGA.
8. Reverse.fashion — AI for sorting and digitizing discarded textiles
Berlin · Seven-figure pre-seed (Jul 2026)
What they do
  • Sell to textile recyclers and circular-economy logistics operators.
  • Applies AI to automate sorting and digitization of discarded textiles for circular-economy logistics.
  • Aims to improve efficiency and profitability in reverse operations.
The problem they solve
  • Textile sorting is manual, slow, and inaccurate. Most clothes go to landfill.
  • EU Extended Producer Responsibility regulations mandate textile recycling.
  • Example: a recycler classifies 10x more garments per hour with AI vision sorting.
Money
  • B2B — SaaS + hardware integration.
  • Led by High-Tech Gründerfonds (HTGF).
Moat
For:
  • EU regulation is creating mandatory demand for textile sorting tech.
  • Proprietary training data from real sorting lines compounds over time.
Against:
  • Sorting robots (ZenRobotics, Picvisa) already operate in waste.
  • Pre-seed capital limits ability to deploy hardware at scale.
Verdict: 🟡 Regulatory tailwind is real. Very early stage.
Crowding
  • ZenRobotics, Picvisa, Refiberd, Fibersort.
  • Low in AI-first textile digitization. Moderate in waste sorting broadly.
