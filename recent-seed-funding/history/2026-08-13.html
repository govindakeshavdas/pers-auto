1. deeplify 🟢 — AI defect detection for industrial inspection (pipelines, tanks, pressure vessels)
deeplify.de · Bochum, Germany · €2M pre-seed (Mar 2026)
What they do
  • Sell to inspection firms and asset owners in energy, oil & gas, chemicals.
  • Replace Excel-and-PDF inspection workflows with one AI pipeline.
  • Ingest raw NDT sensor data and imagery, run automated defect detection.
  • Output auditable inspection reports with full digital traceability.
The problem they solve
  • A pipeline operator sends an inspector. He records ultrasound data by hand.
  • The report takes days. Errors cause unnecessary shutdowns or missed cracks.
  • deeplify claims 70% less inspection time and 66% fewer reporting errors.
Fit
Objective: A crack is there or it isn't. Measured against physical specs.
Start: NDT data formats (DICONDE) are public standards. Build a classifier week one.
Moat: Labeled defect corpus across NDT modalities. Grows with every inspection.
US slot: Open. German company; no funded US startup does AI-native NDT software.
Verdict: 🟢 Technically meaty. Sells the outcome — pass/fail on the asset.
Crowding
  • Gecko Robotics ($173M) inspects with robots but sells hardware + services.
  • Eddyfi and Olympus sell NDT instruments, not AI analysis software.
  • No funded US seed doing the pure software layer for NDT defect analysis.
2. Kebra 🟢 — AI that documents field service jobs before the truck leaves the driveway
kebra.com · San Francisco · YC S26
What they do
  • Sell to HVAC, plumbing, and electrical field service companies.
  • Capture job details from techs via photos, voice, and text — no app install.
  • Auto-generate job notes, warranty claims, parts orders, and sales follow-ups.
  • Build a "Company Brain" of accumulated tribal knowledge from every job.
The problem they solve
  • A tech fixes an AC unit. He texts "done" and drives off.
  • The office spends an hour chasing him for the serial number and photos.
  • Kebra extracts structured data on-site. The warranty claim files itself.
Fit
Objective: Serial numbers, parts, warranty filings — all checkable facts.
Start: SMS + multimodal extraction. Build with Twilio + vision API, week one.
Moat: Company Brain — tribal knowledge from every resolved escalation. Compounds.
US slot: Thin. ServiceTitan does FSM, not AI documentation. XOi does video, not text.
Verdict: 🟢 Sells the outcome — recovered warranty dollars and filed claims.
Crowding
  • ServiceTitan ($9.5B) is the FSM incumbent. Doesn't do AI job capture.
  • XOi Technologies does video capture for techs. Different modality.
  • No funded seed competitor doing multimodal AI documentation for field service.

4. AgentMail 🟡 — Email inboxes built for AI agents, not humans
agentmail.to · San Francisco · $6M seed (Mar 2026)
What they do
  • API that creates a real email inbox for an AI agent in one call.
  • Two-way email: send, receive, parse, thread, label, search, reply.
  • Integrates with LangChain, LlamaIndex, CrewAI out of the box.
  • 500+ B2B customers and hundreds of thousands of agent accounts.
The problem they solve
  • An AI agent needs to confirm a hotel booking. It needs a real email address.
  • Gmail rate-limits API access. SendGrid is send-only. Neither handles threads.
  • AgentMail gives the agent a full inbox — no OAuth, no human in the loop.
Fit
Objective: Email delivered or bounced. Inbox created or not. Binary.
Start: SMTP is public protocol. Build MTA + API alone in week one.
Moat: Deliverability reputation and agent identity network. But email infra is commodity.
US slot: First mover. No direct competitor for agent-specific email yet.
Verdict: 🟡 Passes. Risk: SendGrid or Resend adds an agent-inbox endpoint and it's over.
Crowding
  • SendGrid (Twilio), Resend, Mailgun all do email APIs. None target agents yet.
  • No funded startup doing agent-specific email. First mover for now.
