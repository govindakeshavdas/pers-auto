1. GhostEye 🔴 — AI social-engineering pentest platform
cybersecurity · New York, NY · founded 2025 · pre-seed (YC S25) · ~3 people · ghosteye.ai · YC page

What they do
  • Autonomous AI agents simulate social engineering attacks against employees.
  • Deploys phishing emails, scam calls, SMS, and deepfakes to test workforce resilience.
  • Tracks individual risk scores; delivers targeted training per employee.
  • Uses OSINT to gather public info about the org and its people.
The problem they solve
  • Traditional security training is checkbox compliance; employees forget it in weeks.
  • Vishing attacks grew 442% in 2024, driven by voice cloning and AI impersonation.
  • Example: an attacker clones the CFO's voice, calls a finance analyst, requests a wire transfer. GhostEye runs that same scenario first to see who falls for it.
Money
  • Offers a discounted 48-hour assessment as launch deal.
  • Likely engagement-based or subscription pricing — not disclosed.
  • Team of 3, YC S25 batch. No external funding beyond YC announced.
Why now
  • Cheap voice cloning + LLM-written phishing makes attacks far more convincing than 2 years ago.
  • $40B in AI-enabled fraud expected in the US by 2027.
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: Objective — did the employee click/respond/hand over creds? Measurable. ✅
  • LLM deleted: OSINT scraper + campaign runner. Red-team consultancies already do this manually. 🔴
  • Accumulates: Not assessed — stopped at gate 2.
  • Week one: Not assessed.
  • Buyer: Not assessed.
Verdict: 🔴 Gate 2. Founders met at BlackRock, where one was on the Red Team. A pentesting firm hires one dev to wrap LLM calls around their existing red-team playbooks and beats me. Domain experts own this space. (YC S25 — flagged.)
Crowding
  • KnowBe4 (public, dominant in phishing simulation), Proofpoint, Cofense, Hoxhunt.
  • VanishID emulates attacker recon; ZeroFox covers social-media phishing.
2. nao Labs 🔴 — Open-source AI analytics agent for data teams
data analytics · Paris, France · founded 2024 · pre-seed (YC X25) · small team · getnao.io · GitHub

What they do
  • Open-source framework to build and deploy analytics agents.
  • Users ask questions in natural language; the agent generates SQL, runs queries, visualizes results.
  • Unit-test your agent's performance before deploying.
  • Enterprise edition adds white-label branding, SSO, advanced permissions.
The problem they solve
  • In every company, data teams were a bottleneck to create insights from data.
  • Example: a product manager wants "weekly retention by cohort." Today they file a Jira ticket and wait two days. With nao, they type the question and get SQL + chart.
Money
  • Open-source core (Apache 2.0) + paid enterprise tier.
  • Enterprise adds SSO/OIDC, custom branding, Entra auth.
  • YC X25 batch; no disclosed external funding beyond YC.
Why now
  • LLMs are now good enough at SQL generation that text-to-SQL works on real schemas.
  • Context engineering is a new discipline. Nobody has all the answers yet.
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: Objective — SQL output is correct or not; eval harness measures it. ✅
  • LLM deleted: Context CLI + eval framework + deployment tooling. Technical system. ✅
  • Accumulates: Nothing durable. Snowflake Cortex, Databricks Assistant, every warehouse is adding this. 🔴
  • Week one: Not assessed — stopped at gate 3.
  • Buyer: Not assessed.
Verdict: 🔴 Gate 3. Text-to-SQL analytics is being absorbed as a platform feature by every major data warehouse. Open-source means anyone can fork. It positions itself closer to a software dev lifecycle for analytics agents than a simple text-to-SQL wrapper — but that distinction won't survive platform absorption. Also: founded 2024, borderline on year filter. (YC X25 — flagged.)
Crowding

  • Indie/startup layer: Delphi, Defog, Waii, DataChat, numerous open-source text-to-SQL projects.

Not YC (good), but founded 2024 (borderline on year filter).

4. Mount 🔴 — AI agent liability insurance carrier
insurtech · San Francisco, CA · founded 2025 · pre-seed (YC S26) · 2 people · YC page

What they do
  • Sells liability insurance for companies deploying AI agents. Scans and red-teams the agent workflow, reduces failure modes, insures the residual risk.
  • Three steps: scan for vulnerabilities, improve security, then insure with 48-hour activation and dynamic pricing.
  • Developing "ADR certification" — aims to be the SOC 2 equivalent for AI agents.
The problem they solve
  • Legacy insurance often excludes autonomous software; traditional insurers lack data to assess AI systems.
  • Example: a company deploys an AI agent that handles customer refunds. The agent hallucinates a policy and issues $200K in unauthorized refunds. No existing policy clearly covers that.
Money
  • Insurance premiums + security scanning fees.
  • Two-person team. Amherd handles product and underwriting; Bachmann runs ops and growth.
  • YC Spring 2026 batch. Actively hiring go-to-market, AI engineering, underwriting.
Why now
  • AI agents now write code, handle customer data, move money, and update records — creating liability no existing policy covers.
  • Open debate: will AI liability become a standalone product category, or stay embedded in cyber/E&O?
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: Risk scoring is actuarial — objective enough. ✅
  • LLM deleted: An underwriting model + red-team scanner. Insurance domain expertise required. 🔴
  • Accumulates: Not assessed — stopped at gate 2.
  • Week one: Not assessed.
  • Buyer: Not assessed, but also 🔴 — insurance is a regulated, capital-intensive business built on demonstrated capacity and legal authority to bear risk.
Verdict: 🔴 Gates 2 + 5. Insurance requires actuarial expertise, carrier licenses, capital reserves, and reinsurance relationships. A bootstrapper cannot enter this space. Even the YC backing is barely enough — whether Mount becomes the category leader will depend on regulatory clarity and carrier partnerships. Interesting signal that AI-agent liability is a real gap, but impossible to bootstrap. (YC S26 — flagged.)
Crowding
  • Klaimee (also YC S26) markets AI agent liability insurance with EU AI Act focus.
  • Corgi is a broader startup insurance carrier where AI coverage is one module.
