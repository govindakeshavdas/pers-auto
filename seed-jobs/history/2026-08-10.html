1. Pangram Labs 🟡 — Detects AI-generated text with measurable accuracy claims
pangram.com · Brooklyn, NY · 2023 · Seed (~$14M total) · ~8 people
What they do
  • Classifies text as human-written, AI-generated, or mixed.
  • Ships a web detector, browser extension, and API.
  • Claims "over 99.98% accuracy" via NLP pattern analysis.
The problem they solve
  • A professor gets 200 student essays. Which ones did ChatGPT write?
  • Universities struggle to address misuse of AI. Pangram builds partnerships in higher ed.
  • Also serves businesses and media orgs verifying product reviews and news.
Money
  • SaaS API + browser extension for institutions and enterprises.
  • Raised $9M in July 2026, led by Menlo Ventures.
  • Total funding now ~$13–14M over 3 rounds.
Why now
  • LLM output is now indistinguishable from human text to a reader.
  • Detection requires constant retraining as model families evolve.
Bootstrap fit
Objective: Binary: AI or not. Round-trip measurable against known-provenance text. ✅
LLM deleted: Detection models, training data, and eval harness remain. ✅
Accumulates: Training corpus of labeled AI/human text grows with each model gen. ✅
Week one: Customer submits text, gets classification. Works day one. ✅
Buyer: EdTech procurement at universities. Often RFP-gated, slow cycles. ⚠️
Verdict: 🟡 — Real tech moat in the detection models. But the buyer (university procurement) is slow, Turnitin is entrenched, and $14M of VC means they're already past the scrappy phase. The erosion risk: model providers could ship detection as a feature.
Crowding
  • Top competitors include GPTZero, Copyleaks, and Originality.AI.
  • Turnitin ($1.7B revenue) added AI detection. Pangram is the indie challenger.
