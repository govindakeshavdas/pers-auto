1. Merchantee 🟡 — Automated repricing and campaign execution for marketplace sellers
merchantee.com · Prague, Czech Republic · 2023 · Pre-seed/Seed (~€2M total) · ~10 people
What they do
  • Reprices products across European marketplaces every 30–60 minutes.
  • Automates ad/promo campaigns per product based on budget, performance, market potential.
  • Consolidates catalogue, pricing, campaign data across Allegro, Amazon, Kaufland, etc.
The problem they solve
  • A seller with 5,000 SKUs on 4 marketplaces cannot manually adjust prices hourly.
  • Many retailers hit a growth limit because the number of decisions explodes. Merchantee handles that operational complexity.
  • Used by Philips, Lindt, SodaStream, and Vilgain , plus hundreds of smaller sellers.
Money
  • SaaS subscription per seller account.
  • Successful balancing of expansion and continuous profitability.
  • Total funding: ~$2M. Founded 2023.
Why now
  • 60%+ of European e-commerce volume now runs through marketplaces like Amazon, Allegro, etc.
  • AI models cheap enough to run SKU-level repricing + campaign optimization at high frequency.
Bootstrap fit
Objective: Price adjustments follow seller-set rules + competitor prices. Checkable.
LLM deleted: Repricing engine, API connectors, campaign logic remain. Technical system.
Accumulates: Each marketplace connector (Allegro, eMag, BOL, Cdiscount) is hard-won.
Week one: Seller connects marketplace account; system pulls data and starts repricing.
Buyer: E-commerce ops managers. Mid-market. Reachable, paying today.
Verdict: 🟡 — Passes all five, but founder Jakub Vraspír launched Mall.cz, the first marketplace in Czechia . Domain-rooted relationships + European marketplace knowledge give incumbents a structural head start over a bootstrapper entering cold. The connectors accumulate, but the GTM channel into European sellers is relationship-gated.
Crowding
  • Feedvisor (Amazon-focused, US, raised $70M+), ChannelEngine (NL, Series B), Seller Labs, Profitero.
  • Most competitors focus on Amazon; European multi-marketplace is less crowded.

3. Lantern 🔴 — ML demand forecasting for wholesale distributors
lanternhq.com · San Francisco · 2024 · Seed ($7M) · ~6 people
What they do
  • Automated, ML-driven recommendations: what to buy, how much, when to order.
  • Recognizes cross-SKU and regional dependencies beyond rolling averages or static rules.
  • Extends to lead time prediction, order frequency, safety stock, bulk buying decisions.
The problem they solve
  • "Every distributor we talk to is running inventory decisions on tribal knowledge and gut feel."
  • A distributor with 30,000 SKUs ties up millions in excess if they overbuy.
  • Named customer: Schaedler Yesco — "workflows are faster, cleaner, smarter with Lantern."
Money
  • SaaS sold to wholesale distribution purchasing teams.
  • $7M seed led by Primary Venture Partners and Pear VC.
  • Customers using Lantern have achieved 10% higher sales.
Why now
  • "Only giants like Amazon and Walmart have this tech. We're democratizing it."
  • ML inference cheap enough to run per-SKU, per-branch forecasting at scale.
Bootstrap fit
Objective: Forecast accuracy is measurable against actual demand. Passes.
LLM deleted: ML models, data pipelines, ERP connectors remain. Technical system. Passes.
Accumulates: Forecasting models improve with data; ERP integrations accumulate. Passes.
Week one: 🔴 — Needs distributor's historical sales data + ERP integration. Model trains on their history. Customer gets nothing until data flows in and model converges. Cold start.
Buyer: Not reached.
Verdict: 🔴 — Gate 4. Value requires historical purchasing data, ERP integration (often custom), and model training. Useless on day one without the distributor's data. Strong traction signal — founders interviewed 200+ distributors at Stanford — but that's domain depth I don't have, compounding the problem.
Crowding
  • Blue Yonder (SAP-adjacent), EazyStock, Lokad — mature forecasting tools but all legacy/enterprise.
  • Few distribution-specific ML forecasting startups. The niche is narrow enough to matter.
4. Channel3 🔴 — Universal product graph for AI-driven shopping (YC — included for lack of non-YC equivalent)
trychannel3.com · New York · 2025 · Seed ($6M) · ~2–5 people
What they do
  • Building a universal, AI-ready product graph — a connected database of every product on the internet.
  • Developers use the API to power AI-driven shopping: discover, compare, route users to products.
  • Catalog has 50M+ products and expanding daily.
The problem they solve
  • Devs building agentic shopping apps get stuck on product data — collecting it is too hard. Channel3 handles the infra layer.
  • Example: an AI stylist app needs to know every variant of a jacket across 50 merchants.
Money
  • API access + affiliate commission infrastructure for developers.
  • $6M seed led by Matrix, with Paul Graham, Sri Batchu (ex-TheRealReal CMO) as angels.
  • Powers hundreds of developers building AI shopping experiences.
Why now
  • Multimodal AI models now smart enough to understand products and cheap enough to operate at scale.
  • AI shopping agents (ChatGPT, Gemini) need structured product data to work.
Bootstrap fit
Objective: Product matching and data structuring are checkable (is product X on merchant Y correct?). Passes.
LLM deleted: The product graph, crawlers, matching pipelines remain. Technical system. Passes.
Accumulates: The 50M-product graph itself. Each crawled and matched product adds value. Passes.
Week one: 🔴 — Customer #1 needs the graph to already exist. Building 50M products before first sale is a massive cold-start. Data-network-effect business.
Buyer: Not reached.
Verdict: 🔴 — Gate 4. Classic data-network-effect cold start: the product graph must exist before any developer gets value. A solo founder cannot crawl, match, and structure 50M products before customer #1. This is a venture bet, not a bootstrap.
Crowding
  • Google Shopping feed, Amazon Product API own the incumbent data. Dataweave, Salsify are tangential.
  • Shopify and BigCommerce are layering AI. Competition could intensify quickly.
