1. Vals AI   🟢 Bootstrap fit
vals.ai · San Francisco · Founded 2024 · Seed (~$5M) · ~12 employees

Vals AI is an independent, third-party AI model evaluation company that builds and publishes domain-specific benchmarks measuring how well large language models perform on real professional tasks — concentrating on high-stakes knowledge work in fields such as law, tax, finance, and healthcare, often constructing its evaluation sets with domain experts. Concretely: an enterprise customer says "we're choosing between GPT-5, Claude Opus, and Gemini for our contract-review pipeline." Vals hosts leaderboards that compare LLMs with respect to accuracy, cost, and speed, with analysis of the results. They collaborated with financial industry experts to develop a Finance Agent Benchmark; the best-performing model (OpenAI's o3) achieved only 46.8% accuracy at $3.79 per query. Revenue model is SaaS evaluation infrastructure sold to AI labs and enterprise engineering teams, plus private custom benchmark engagements. Reported $1.3M revenue and 12-person team in 2025. The "why now" is straightforward: every enterprise deploying LLMs needs to pick a model per task, and generic benchmarks (MMLU, etc.) don't reflect domain performance — Vals evaluates models on the real tasks each industry relies on.

Bootstrap fit
Objectivity: Output is a score (accuracy %) on a benchmark with a known answer key — mechanically checkable. Passes.
Without LLM: What remains is the benchmark datasets, the evaluation harness, and the scoring pipeline. This is a measurement system, not domain knowledge. Passes.
Durability: The private, curated question sets (constructed with experts, held back from training data) accumulate. They use private, secure datasets to prevent data leakage, curated to avoid contamination from publicly available training data. Each new benchmark deepens the moat. The harness and dataset library are the thing that accumulates.
Day-one value: Customer #1 gets a leaderboard comparison across models for their task class on day one — the benchmarks exist before the customer arrives. No cold start.
Buyer: AI labs and enterprise engineering teams making model-selection decisions. Reachable via dev-marketing, public leaderboards as top-of-funnel. Willingness to pay is demonstrated (Vals already has revenue).
Verdict: 🟢 — The curated private evaluation datasets and the scoring harness accumulate.

Crowding: Competitors include general-purpose eval platforms (Braintrust, Patronus AI, Galileo), but Vals differentiates with domain-specific, private-question benchmarks in regulated verticals. Arthur AI does model monitoring but not industry-specific benchmarking. The space is growing but not yet crowded with domain-specific players. Not YC-backed.

2. BenchSpan   🟢 Bootstrap fit
YC P26 · San Francisco · Founded 2026 · 2 employees · (YC P26 — included for lack of non-YC equivalent in agent benchmarking infra)

BenchSpan is a benchmarking platform for AI agents. Running benchmarks today is slow, expensive, and fragile — teams spend days writing glue code for each new benchmark, runs take forever on a laptop, and partial failures waste hundreds of dollars in tokens. BenchSpan fixes this: onboard your agent once and it works with every benchmark on the platform. They onboarded Claude Code in 37 lines of code. Running a benchmark becomes a single command executed in parallel in the cloud, with every result going to one place the whole team can see — full trajectories, token usage, latency, and custom metrics. Partial-failure reruns and side-by-side comparison are built in. The product is infrastructure — a benchmark execution engine — not the benchmarks themselves. The customer is any team building or shipping an AI agent who needs regression testing across standard benchmarks. Revenue model is likely usage-based SaaS.

Bootstrap fit
Objectivity: Output is benchmark scores — pass/fail, accuracy %, token cost — all mechanically measured against known test sets. Passes.
Without LLM: What's left is the execution harness, the SDK adapters for each benchmark, the parallelized cloud runner, and the results dashboard. This is a technical system, not domain knowledge. Passes.
Durability: The library of benchmark integrations (each requiring custom adapter work), the execution infrastructure, and the accumulated results database (enabling trend/regression analysis) all accumulate. Each new benchmark added deepens the integration moat.
Day-one value: Customer points their agent at BenchSpan, runs an existing public benchmark in the cloud, gets results in minutes. Zero historical data required.
Buyer: AI engineering teams at startups and mid-market companies shipping agents. Reachable through dev communities, HN, GitHub. Strong willingness to pay (saves expensive token burn and eng time).
Verdict: 🟢 — The benchmark adapter library and execution infrastructure accumulate. However, the YC backing makes this a tough competitive target.

Crowding: Other startups in the agent testing/benchmarking space in the same YC batch include TesterArmy (browser QA testing), Armature ("test agent flows"), Arga Labs (code validation), Chronicle Labs (backtest vs prod), and Bento Labs (monitoring long-running agents). Vals AI (above) does model-level benchmarking but not agent-level execution harnesses. Braintrust and Langsmith offer eval features but not dedicated benchmark-as-a-service. Space is early and fragmented.

3. Spur   🟡 Bootstrap fit
spurtest.com · New York · Founded 2024 · Seed ($5M) · ~8 employees · (YC S24 — included as the defining company in agent-based QA, no non-YC equivalent at this traction)

Spur uses AI agents to test websites for bugs — users can instruct the system to "add a product to the cart" or "submit a job application," and the agent will test those website flows. Technically, the platform features AI-driven browser agents that emulate real user interactions without reliance on CSS selectors or code access, supports natural language test creation, multi-step and multi-tab workflows, dynamic form testing, multilingual capabilities, and secure data handling. Key customers include NextDoor, LivingSpaces, and Wander.com. One customer went from "0 to 80% coverage in 1 month." Revenue model is SaaS, likely per-test-run or per-site. The "why now" is that browser-use agents crossed a reliability threshold in 2024–2025, making it possible to write a test in natural language and have an agent actually execute it through arbitrary web UI.

Bootstrap fit
Objectivity: Output is "does this UI flow work or not" — binary pass/fail against expected behavior. Mechanically checkable. Passes.
Without LLM: What remains is the browser agent harness (synthetic user engine), the test recorder, the Slack integration, the regression framework. A technical system. Passes.
Durability: The browser agent itself is the moat — but browser-use agents are rapidly improving in open-source (Playwright + LLM wrappers, etc.). Major cloud testing incumbents (BrowserStack, Sauce Labs) and new entrants could replicate the core loop. The accumulated agent reliability across edge cases and the test-recording corpus help, but the barrier erodes as browser-use APIs commoditize.
Day-one value: Customer records a flow or types a natural-language instruction, Spur runs it. Works immediately, no historical data needed. Passes.
Buyer: Engineering and QA teams at e-commerce and SaaS companies. Reachable, willing to pay. Passes.
Verdict: 🟡 — Real depth and product-market fit, but the browser-agent layer is commoditizing fast. The moat is reliability and breadth of site compatibility, which accumulates slowly but can be replicated by well-funded testing incumbents.

Crowding: QA Wolf (funded, scaled), Rainforest QA (sunset/acquired), Cypress (open-source), Playwright (open-source), and now AI-native entrants like TesterArmy (YC P26). BrowserStack and LambdaTest could add natural-language test features. Growing but competitive.

Bootstrap fit

Durability: The document processing pipeline and the fine-tuned redaction models accumulate accuracy over time. However, eDiscovery is a mature market with DISCO, Relativity, and Everlaw all adding AI redaction features. A single-function redaction tool risks absorption. Erodes, but the Fortune 500 relationships help.
Day-one value: Customer uploads documents, defines rules, gets redacted output. Works immediately. Passes.
Buyer: In-house legal teams at large companies. These are somewhat procurement-gated, but Phaselab's Fortune 500 traction suggests the buyer is reachable. Marginal pass — the enterprise sales cycle is long.
Verdict: 🟡 — Real depth, Fortune 500 customers, but eDiscovery incumbents will absorb AI redaction as a feature. The enterprise sales cycle also makes bootstrapping harder.

Crowding: DISCO, Relativity, Everlaw, iDox.ai, Onna, and Exterro all play in eDiscovery/document review. AI-native redaction is a feature being added by all incumbents. The space is mature and competitive. Not YC.
