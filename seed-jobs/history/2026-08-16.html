1. Fabrion 🔴 — AI operating system for industrial manufacturers
Manufacturing AI · San Francisco · Founded 2025 · Seed (~$10.5M from 8VC) · ~8 people
fabrion.com · job posting
What they do
  • Connects fragmented data across pre-production, production, and post-production via a knowledge graph.
  • Full-stack vertical AI: data connectors, ontologies, graph-based data fabrics, agent meshes, fine-tuned models.
  • Targets supply chain agility, launch readiness, and end-of-life planning.
  • Currently operating in stealth mode.
The problem they solve
  • Manufacturing runs on spreadsheets and disconnected systems; tools don't talk; signals get buried in inboxes; decisions made on stale data.
  • Example: a multi-tier auto supplier tracking parts across stamping plants in Ohio and battery lines in Korea — no single view of launch readiness.
  • Result: delayed launches, tied-up working capital, crushed margins.
Money
  • Raised $10.5M; 8VC is the sole disclosed investor.
  • Enterprise SaaS model (pricing not public).
  • $10.5M is slightly above the $10M filter — borderline but included for completeness.
Why now
  • LLMs can now reason over unstructured manufacturing docs (specs, supplier emails) — unlocks the "knowledge graph + agents" architecture.
  • 8VC frames this as a Palantir-style thesis: raw data isn't enough, you need to map how a business operates.
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: Objective in principle (part tracking, launch status), but operational insight is judgment-heavy.
  • LLM deleted: 🔴 What's left is manufacturing domain knowledge — ontologies, BOM structures, compliance rules. A domain firm hires one dev.
  • Accumulates: Not reached — stopped at gate 2.
  • Week one: Not reached — but would also fail; needs live integrations into ERP, PLM, supplier systems.
  • Buyer: Not reached — enterprise procurement-gated.
Verdict: 🔴 — Fails gate 2. Delete the LLM and you have manufacturing domain knowledge. Matches calibration: "AI manufacturing engineer for factories — wrapper + domain experts own it." Also fails gate 4 (cold start: needs ERP/PLM integrations and supplier data) and gate 5 (procurement-gated enterprise buyers).
2. InQuery 🔴 — AI medical record review for legal & insurance (YC)
Medical-legal AI · New York · Founded 2023 · Pre-seed/Seed (~$3.1M) · ~7 people
inquery.ai · job posting
⚠️ Filter note: Founded in 2023 — fails the 2025/2026 founding-year filter. YC-backed. Included for completeness since the shortlist has only four companies.

What they do
  • Uses OCR and NLP to extract dates, diagnoses, treatments, and provider info from medical records.
  • Extracts key diagnoses, treatments, and timeline events, linked to source pages for defensibility.
  • Every output includes source-linked citations and a human QA layer.
  • Purpose-built for PI, med mal, and insurance defense with HIPAA-compliant processing.
The problem they solve
  • Law firms handling PI, med mal, mass torts are buried in PDFs and handwritten notes; manual review creates bottlenecks.
  • Example: a PI paralegal with 2,000 pages of medical records across 8 providers — needs a chronology for a demand letter by Friday.
  • Pricing in the space: $0.50–$2.00 per page for AI-assisted review; high-volume cases get expensive.
Money
  • Raised $3.1M from Y Combinator, General Catalyst, DRW Venture Capital, and others.
  • Per-page or subscription pricing (typical for the category).
  • 7 employees as of mid-2026.
Why now
  • LLMs can now read messy clinical handwriting and multi-format EHR exports accurately enough for first-pass extraction.
  • Source-linking — every fact clickable to a Bates-stamped page — is what makes AI output defensible at deposition.
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: Extraction is mostly objective (dates, codes, providers). Passes gate 1.
  • LLM deleted: 🔴 What's left is medical-legal domain knowledge — understanding what matters in a PI chronology, which billing codes signal upcoding, what adjusters need. A law firm or TPA hires one dev.
  • Accumulates: Not reached.
  • Week one: Not reached — but would pass (customer uploads PDFs, gets chronology).
  • Buyer: Not reached — but reachable (paralegals, adjusters, midsize firms).
Verdict: 🔴 — Fails year filter (2023) and is YC-backed. On the rubric, fails gate 2: delete the LLM and the value is medical-legal domain expertise (what to extract, how to structure a chronology for litigation). Strong traction signal — Stanford CS founders with real customers — but that same "domain expert hires one engineer" pattern. Also overlaps calibration: "Insurance-defense litigation intelligence — needs a case corpus."
Crowding
  • Wisedocs focuses on insurance carriers and TPAs with high-volume intake processing.
  • DigitalOwl offers a self-serve pricing tier for smaller volumes. Also xCures (just raised $46M Series B), plus several smaller players.
  • InQuery has 156 active competitors per Tracxn.
3. Loop AI 🔴 — Delivery intelligence for restaurant back offices
Restaurant tech · New York / San Francisco · Founded 2022 · Series A (~$20M total) · ~30+ people
loopai.com · job posting
⚠️ Filter failures: Founded in 2022 — fails the 2025/2026 year filter. Raised $14M in a Series A ( $20M total ) — fails the pre-seed/seed and under-$10M filters. Dropped on filters alone; rubric applied for completeness.

What they do
  • Automates financial reconciliation for 3rd-party delivery — files journal entries for revenue automatically.
  • AI scans customer reviews and feedback for errors; generates summaries for operators.
  • Provides financial intelligence and operational levers to reduce chargebacks and increase uptime.
The problem they solve
  • Many customers see 30–40% of revenue from 3PD delivery, but fees are a blackbox and reconciliation takes hours per location.
  • Example: a 50-location burger chain reconciling DoorDash, UberEats, and Grubhub payouts against POS — currently done in spreadsheets.
Money
  • Launched 2024; supports 300+ brands and thousands of locations. Raised $6M seed in March 2024.
  • Business increased sixfold since 2024. Clients include Little Caesars, McDonald's, Whataburger.
Why now
  • 3PD delivery volume exploded post-COVID; restaurants now have 3–4 delivery platforms to reconcile.
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: Financial reconciliation is objective (numbers match or don't). Passes.
  • LLM deleted: What's left is a reconciliation engine + delivery-platform integrations. Technical, not domain-knowledge.
  • Accumulates: Integration connectors and reconciliation rules accumulate. Passes in theory.
  • Week one: 🔴 Needs live integrations into POS, 3PD platforms (DoorDash, UberEats), and bank/ERP data. Cold start — useless without connected data feeds.
  • Buyer: Multi-unit restaurant operators. Reachable but relationship-heavy at the enterprise tier they target.
Verdict: 🔴 — Fails multiple entry filters (founded 2022, Series A, $20M raised). On the rubric, fails gate 4: value requires live integrations into POS and delivery platforms. Also, at 300+ brands and enterprise clients like McDonald's, this is far past bootstrappable competitive range.
