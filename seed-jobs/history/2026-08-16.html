1. Booko 🔴 — Dynamic pricing engine for bookable time slots
booko.com · San Francisco · founded 2025 · seed (~$4.3M, YC W26) · ~2 people · YC page · job
What they do
  • Helps businesses that sell bookable time slots make more money through dynamic pricing.
  • Integrates with existing booking systems to adjust prices based on historical utilization, availability, and demand.
  • Forecasts demand class by class and slot by slot, moves price and incentives in real time.
  • Live in production with national fitness brands, boutique studios, clinics, and chains.
The problem they solve
  • Unlike physical inventory, unsold time slots disappear forever, yet most businesses still use static pricing.
  • Example: a yoga studio has 8 open mats at 2 pm Tuesday. Booko drops the price to fill them, like airline yield management.
  • Early customers see ~20% revenue uplift from selling time that would have otherwise gone unsold.
Money
  • Rev share or SaaS on uplift — revenue model not publicly detailed.
  • Raised ~$4.3M. Backed by Y Combinator (W26).
Why now
  • Service economy has digitized marketing, payments, and booking, but not pricing intelligence.
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: Revenue uplift is measurable — not subjective. Passes.
  • LLM deleted: Core is demand-forecast model + booking-API integration. Technical, not domain. Passes.
  • Accumulates: Nothing before customer data arrives. Thin AI wrapper on existing booking APIs with no proprietary data advantage at early stage. 🔴
  • Week one: Needs customer's historical booking history to forecast. Pricing intelligence improves as booking data compounds. Cold start. 🔴
  • Buyer: SMB owners (salon, gym, studio) — fragmented, low ACV, hard to reach at scale.
Verdict: 🔴 — Fails gates 3 + 4. No asset accumulates before customer data arrives. Booking platforms (Mindbody, Calendly) can add this as a feature. Data-network-effect business = bad bootstrap. YC W26.
Crowding
  • Yield management software exists for hospitality (IDeaS, Duetto).
  • Mindbody itself could add dynamic pricing, as could Calendly/Acuity.
2. Pharosyn 🔴 — AI competitive intelligence for pharma
pharosyn.ai · London, UK · founded 2025 · pre-seed (Entrepreneurs First) · ~3 people · site · job
What they do
  • Uses AI to continuously write strategic reports on clinical, regulatory, and commercial events involving competitors.
  • Monitors clinical trials, regulatory filings, press releases, and earnings call transcripts.
  • AI triaging engine scores and ranks every incoming event against the customer's pipeline criteria.
The problem they solve
  • Replaces slow manual reporting with real-time AI-powered competitive intelligence in regulated pharma.
  • Example: a generics company tracks 40 competitor molecules across FDA, EMA, clinical-trial registries. Today that's a CI analyst reading filings manually.
Money
  • Enterprise SaaS to pharma CI teams. No revenue figures public.
  • Has not raised any funding yet. Backed by Entrepreneurs First.
Why now
  • LLMs can now summarize and triage regulatory filings at scale.
  • Manual CI teams spend most time on collection, not analysis.
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: Output is strategic reports with "impact assessments" and event prioritization. That is expert judgment. 🔴
  • LLM deleted: Pharma regulatory + clinical-trial domain knowledge is the core asset. A CI consultancy could hire one dev.
  • Accumulates: Not reached — stopped at gate 1.
  • Week one: Not reached.
  • Buyer: Enterprise pharma — procurement-gated.
Verdict: 🔴 — Fails gate 1. Strategic CI reports are inherently subjective. Also fails gate 2: pharma domain expertise owns the value. Not YC — backed by Entrepreneurs First.
Crowding
  • 117 active competitors including Manifold AI, Komodo Health, and Owkin.
  • Patsnap Eureka, established CI consultancies, and internal pharma CI teams all compete here.
3. Weave 🔴 — AI measurement of engineering team productivity
workweave.dev · San Francisco · founded 2025 · seed (~$4.2M, YC W25) · ~4 people · YC page · job
What they do
  • Uses AI to measure engineering work — runs LLMs and custom models on every PR and review, analyzing output and quality.
  • PR-level code attribution: determines what was written by AI, what wasn't, and what should've been.
  • Key metric is "Weave Hour": how long would it take an experienced engineer to make this change?
The problem they solve
  • Engineering measurement has been impossible, so leaders fly blind — relying on gut feel or shoddy metrics.
  • Example: an eng VP wants to know how much Cursor adoption actually speeds up the team. Weave shows AI-written vs. human-written code per engineer.
Money
  • SaaS, likely per-seat to eng teams.
  • 200+ customers including Robinhood, Rho, PostHog, Browserbase.
  • Raised $4.2M seed from Moonfire, Burst Capital, and YC (W25).
Why now
  • AI coding tools (Cursor, Copilot) create a new measurement need: how much is AI doing?
  • LLMs can now read PRs and estimate effort — not just count lines.
Bootstrap fit
  • Grader: "Productivity," "quality," "how helpful reviews are" — all subjective judgments. "Weave Hour" is an estimate, not a measurement. 🔴
  • LLM deleted: Not reached — stopped at gate 1.
  • Accumulates: Not reached.
  • Week one: Not reached.
  • Buyer: Eng VPs / CTOs — reachable but would need social proof.
Verdict: 🔴 — Fails gate 1. Estimating engineering "quality" and "impact" is inherently subjective — same shape as call-center QA scoring. Good traction signal (200+ customers) but that makes it a worse bootstrap target, not better. YC W25.
Crowding
  • LinearB, Jellyfish, Pluralsight Flow, Haystack, Gitprime (acquired by Pluralsight).
  • GitHub Copilot metrics are native and expanding — platform absorption risk.
