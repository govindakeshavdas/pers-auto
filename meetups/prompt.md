# CONTEXT

I live in the SF Bay Area. Once a month I want a full picture of what is
happening in the local tech / startup / builder scene in the month ahead.

I go to events for two separate reasons, and I want two separate tables — one
per reason. An event can appear in both. Redundancy is fine.

**Reason 1 — builder and general networking.** Business and startup building,
AI, and building software for business or for AI. That is the centre of the
target.

**Reason 2 — selling my AEO business.** AEO is Answer Engine Optimization:
getting a company cited inside AI answers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI
Overviews). I sell to **B2B startups**. The buyer is whoever owns the
marketing budget — head of marketing, head of growth, demand gen, sometimes
the founder. Founders and owners are still worth talking to, as long as the
company is funded enough to have a marketing line. Too early means no budget
and no sale. I sell by giving away a free visibility audit.

Both reasons shape **scoring only**, not searching. Keep searching wide —
every event is worth at least looking at, and I still want the whole board.
Do not narrow your searches to my interests and do not drop things that miss
either centre. Score them honestly and let me decide.

---

# YOUR JOB

Every run:

1. Search the web fresh and find as many real, dated, in-person events as you
   can in the Bay Area (SF, peninsula, East Bay, South Bay) that start on or
   after {{TODAY}} and fall within the following month.
2. Classify each event — what kind of thing it is, and who is actually in the
   room.
3. Score every event on Rubric A. Score it on Rubric B as well if any AEO
   buyer is in the room.
4. Return **everything you found** in Table A, ranked by score. Do not filter.
   Do not trim the list to the good ones. A low score is information, not a
   reason to hide an event from me.
5. Return the sales-worthy subset in Table B, ranked by its own score.

Treat {{TODAY}} as the authoritative current date.

No files. No memory of previous runs. Just search, classify, score, rank.

# HOW TO SEARCH

Work out your own approach. I am not giving you queries, sites, or
communities on purpose — a fixed recipe returns a fixed answer, and I want
the search itself to vary run to run. Come at it from several different
angles rather than one.

Be exhaustive, not efficient. Use your full search budget. Keep searching
until new searches stop surfacing events you have not already found.

A month in the Bay Area has hundreds of real events. **Under 40 means you
stopped early, not that the month was quiet.** Recurring weekly and monthly
series alone should account for a dozen. If your list is short, you have not
finished.

For a recurring series, list the **next actual dated instance** — "Wed Sep 16",
not "every Wednesday". If you cannot confirm a real date in the window, drop
it. A recurring series is not an exception to the date rule below.

Spend a real part of your budget outside the builder scene, or Table B will
be empty. Marketing, growth, demand gen, GTM, revenue ops, product marketing,
SEO and B2B SaaS communities all run their own events, and none of them show
up in startup or AI searches. Founder dinners and funded-startup meetups
count too.

Hard rules:

- Only include events whose start date you can actually confirm. If you
  cannot confirm the date after searching, drop the event rather than guess.
- Every link must be a page you actually saw in search results. Never
  construct, guess, or pattern-match a URL.
- Exclude virtual-only events, and anything that has already happened.
- Exclude nothing else. Not pitch nights, not recruiting events, not big
  corporate ones. Tag them and score them instead.

# CLASSIFY

For each event, give me three things:

**Type** — pick the closest:
hack night / workshop · demo night · talk or panel · conference or summit ·
dinner or salon · mixer or social · pitch or investor event · hiring event ·
recurring user group · trade show or expo · other (say what)

**Room** — one short, honest phrase on who actually shows up. Specific beats
flattering: "~20 people, working engineers", "mostly recruiters and vendors",
"large and mixed", "new event, unknown crowd".

**Scale** — rough expected headcount if you can tell, otherwise "unknown".

# RUBRIC A — BUILDER

Five axes, max 22, then apply the penalty below.

1. **Crowd** (1-5) — Are the people in the room doing the work (builders,
   operators, researchers), or spectating (recruiters, vendors, tourists,
   professional networkers)?
2. **Substance** (1-5) — Will real products, real numbers, and real problems
   get discussed, or is it surface-level takes and small talk?
3. **Fit** (1-5) — How close is this to business and startup building, AI, or
   building software for business or for AI?
   - **5** — squarely on target: AI builders, applied AI, founders building
     companies, tooling for AI or for business.
   - **4** — clearly adjacent: general startup, product, or infra events where
     business or AI is a live topic.
   - **3** — general tech with no particular business or AI angle.
   - **2** — a meetup organised around a single programming language,
     framework, or general dev craft, with no business or AI angle. A monthly
     Python or Rust presentation night is a 2, however good the talks are.
   - **1** — foreign government, consulate, chamber of commerce, trade
     delegation, or country-promotion events. Also pure professional
     networking with nothing being built or sold.
4. **Friction** (1-5) — Cost, timing, travel, RSVP difficulty. Free or cheap
   and reachable anywhere in the Bay scores high. Do not penalise an event for
   being outside San Francisco — South Bay, peninsula and East Bay are all
   fine.
   - I strongly prefer **evening** events. Cap all-day, multi-day and daytime
     events at **2**, regardless of how cheap or close they are.
5. **Novelty** (0-2) — Is this new, unusual, or hard to stumble across? A
   recurring series that runs every single week scores 0; a one-off or a first
   edition scores 2. This is a small tiebreaker only, not a main signal.

No axis ever goes below its stated minimum. Friction is 1 at worst, never 0,
however expensive or awkward the event is.

**Penalty — crypto:** subtract **3** from the total if the event is
substantially about crypto, tokens, web3, blockchain, or digital assets. Apply
it once. Show it in the breakdown as `crypto -3`. This one also applies to
Rubric B.

**Penalty — vendor:** subtract **3** from the total if the event is really a
provider marketing their own product. The tell is that the agenda *is* their
tool. This covers a company's own user conference or summit, a "workshop" or
"hack night" a vendor runs to get people onto their platform, and paid
training courses that exist to sell the next course. A sponsor logo on a real
community event does not count — sponsorship is fine, an agenda owned by one
seller is not. Apply it once. Show it in the breakdown as `vendor -3`.

This one does **not** apply to Rubric B. A vendor's room is still full of
buyers, and that is all Table B cares about.

Show the five sub-scores and any penalties. Do not collapse them into a total
only.

# RUBRIC B — AEO

Three axes, max 15, then the same crypto penalty.

This is a completely separate question from Rubric A. A great builder event is
usually a bad sales event, and the reverse is just as true. Most
engineer-heavy rooms belong nowhere near Table B. Do not soften a low score to
be polite, and do not let a high Rubric A score pull this one up.

1. **Buyers** (1-5) — How many people in the room own or influence the
   marketing budget at a B2B startup?
   - **5** — the room is my buyer: heads of marketing, growth, demand gen,
     product marketing, revenue ops.
   - **4** — founders and operators of funded B2B startups who still own
     marketing themselves.
   - **3** — mixed room, buyers are a real but small minority.
   - **2** — mostly people with no say over marketing spend.
   - **1** — no buyers: students, job seekers, researchers, engineers at
     large companies with a central marketing org.
2. **Budget** (1-5) — Are these companies funded enough to buy?
   - **5** — seed through Series B B2B startups that already spend on
     marketing, or agencies who would resell.
   - **3** — early companies with revenue but no marketing line yet.
   - **1** — pre-seed, idea stage, unfunded, hobbyist, or non-commercial. Too
     early to sell to.
3. **Access** (1-5) — Can I get in front of them cheaply? A speaking slot, a
   demo table, or a room under ~40 with a free ticket is 5. A big seated
   keynote or an expensive or invite-only ticket is 1.

Show the three sub-scores and any penalty.

# SCORING HYGIENE

These apply to both rubrics:

- The total must equal the sum of the sub-scores, minus any penalty. Add them
  up rather than eyeballing. Check the bottom of the table as carefully as the
  top — that is where totals drift.
- Sort each table once, at the end, after every event is scored. Do not sort
  as you go.
- Show the numbers exactly as specified. No asterisks, footnotes, daggers or
  invented markers. If you are unsure about something, say it in the Room
  phrase in words.

# OUTPUT

Return a self-contained **HTML fragment** (no `<html>`, `<head>`, or `<body>`
wrappers — it will be embedded into a Gmail email).

Structure:

1. One short `<p>` reading the month at a glance — how many events you found,
   any pattern worth noting (e.g. "unusually heavy on hiring events", "three
   separate hardware nights"), and one line on where the buyers are.

2. `<h3>` **Everything on**, then an inline-styled `<table>` of **every event

   **Event | Date & time | Type | Room | Score /22 | Link**
   - Score cell shows the total and the breakdown, e.g. `17` then a smaller
     line `crowd 4 · substance 5 · fit 4 · friction 3 · novelty 1`. Append
     `· crypto -3` when the penalty applies.
   - Link text is the domain only, not the full URL.
   - Bold the top row.

3. `<h3>` **Where the buyers are**, then a second inline-styled `<table>`,
   sorted by Rubric B score descending, with columns:
   **Event | Date & time | Who is in the room | Score /15 | Angle | Link**
   - Include any event scoring **8 or more**. If fewer than five clear that
     bar, show the top five anyway so I can see how thin the month is.
   - "Who is in the room" names the roles and the kind of company, not a vague
     label — e.g. "~200 heads of growth at funded B2B SaaS", "~40 founders,
     mostly seed-stage".
   - Score cell shows the total and the breakdown, e.g. `12` then a smaller
     line `buyers 5 · budget 4 · access 3`, with the penalty appended the same

   - "Angle" is one sentence, max 15 words, on what I would actually offer in
     that room. Concrete beats generic: "free AEO audit for three seed-stage
     SaaS founders on the spot" beats "network with marketers". If the event
     has an open call for speakers or a cheap sponsor or demo slot, say so
     here with the deadline instead.
   - Bold the top row.

4. After both tables, one `<p>` each for the top three in Table A: what it is
   and why it scored where it did. Two sentences max each.

Formatting requirements:

- Inline `style="..."` only. No `<style>` blocks. No external CSS.
- Gmail-safe tables: border-collapse, ~8px cell padding, light header
  background, simple 1px borders.
- Table A must include every event you found, however low it scored.

Output **only** the HTML fragment. No preamble, no explanation, no code
fences, no markdown.
