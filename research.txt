3. Forge 🟢 — Recovers unclaimed duty drawback refunds for importers
forgehq.com · San Francisco · YC-backed (pre-seed stage)
What they do
Sell to US importers who later export or scrap goods.
File duty drawback claims with CBP. AI extracts data from customs entries.
White-glove service: customer does near-zero work.
The problem they solve
~$6B+ in eligible refunds go unclaimed yearly. 80% of companies never file.
Process is cumbersome: matching import entries to exports across years.
Post-tariff chaos (IEEPA, Section 122) made the dollar amount much bigger.
Fit
Objective: Refund amount is a number. CBP approves or rejects the claim.
Start: Import/export records are public via ACE. Build matching engine alone.
Moat: Entry-to-export matching dataset across HTS codes. Compounds per client.
US slot: Dutypay, Comstock exist but old-line brokers. No AI-native startup funded.
Verdict: 🟢 Sells the outcome (the refund check). Takes a cut, not a subscription.
Crowding
Alliance International, Dutypay are legacy customs brokers doing drawback.
No venture-backed AI-native competitor doing this for SMB importers.

======[__NO__] Relu  AI-automated design for dental lab cases== 
The valuable part (crowns, surgical guides) is still beta after 7 years; what ships (night guards, models, trays, ditching) is procedural mesh geometry, not defensible AI.
Segmentation — their original edge — is now commoditized by public data (Teeth3DS, ToothFairy) + nnU-Net.
Price is racing to zero: 3Shape Automate is $1.99/crown, offshore CAD houses are $5–20/unit, and 3Shape/exocad own the CAD desktop and can bundle it free.
Pay-per-case at ~$2 with no lock-in means ~$1–3M ARR on 1M cases — consistent with only ~€5M raised in 7 years.
Going direct-to-lab puts them against their own OEM partners (Planmeca, Medialab) who are their real distribution.
Nothing was actually unlocked recently by AI per se . 
=== [__NO__] Prolo — AI procurement for SME construction contractors=== 

Context — Prolo (UK, £4.2M seed) is a WhatsApp buying desk for small contractors: AI + humans source materials from 185 suppliers and lend up to 90 days.

Problem it claims — SME contractors buy manually, pay retail-ish prices, and wait 60–90 days to get paid while suppliers want net 30.

Why the US version is hard — the basket is horizontal but distribution isn't; Home Depot/SRS, QXO/Beacon and ABC Supply just spent ~$35B consolidating and are chasing "Complex Pro" themselves.

Why it may not be a good idea — it's a distributor wearing an AI coat: ~5–15% gross margin, balance-sheet credit risk into a sector that is ~17% of UK insolvencies, and the sourcing half is already commoditised by Kojo/StructShare while the credit half is already owned by Billd.
